## Normative Concept Envelope

<a id="concept-plan-purpose"></a>
### Purpose

Make the kernel sound before another surface is built on it.

`M2` shipped a working coding agent and closed with a green gate. Then three
independent reviews of the integrated `main` found, between them, three shipped
operator workflows that failed in live use, thirteen further high-severity
product defects, fourteen more after the first repair set, and six blocking
false-greens — every one of them while every gate, the whole suite, and the
retained evidence stayed green. The maintainer authorized repairing them on
`main` as a recorded override of the plan-first rule, because each denied an
operator something the product claimed to do. Those repairs shipped. What did
not ship is the protection: no accepted gate locks a single one of the
regression tests they carry, so the next milestone can delete any of them and
stay green.

That is this milestone. `M3` adds no operator capability and no public surface.
It locks the regressions the post-closure reviews produced, implements the three pieces of
recorded debt `M2` closed with rather than carrying them further, makes the
closed gates run by something instead of by a person remembering to, repairs the
test-honesty weaknesses the reviews froze rather than fixed, and decides the
bootstrap runtime floor while that decision is still free.

The reason to spend a milestone here is arithmetic. `M4` puts a language-neutral
protocol on top of this kernel and invites independent clients to depend on it.
Every defect still resident becomes a defect seen through a wire boundary,
reported by someone who cannot read the code, against a surface that has already
made promises. The cheapest moment to find these is now, and the cheapest way to
keep them found is a gate that runs.

The same source and gate execute on Darwin under both ADR 0002 validation pairs
and on Linux under the current pair, exactly as `M2` did. That is milestone
execution evidence, not an operating-system support statement.

The Gate selector cells identify the protected selector files. The gate contract
locks the complete repository-owned invocation, including role, seed, minimum,
exclusion policy, expected test identities and states, and credential channel.

The Outcome 1 counts are derived rather than declared. They are every case the
range `6345ded..0509d5d3` added to a test file, plus every case in a file that
range created, taken at the base this plan opens on — and the override sets were
still landing on `main` while it was written, so the numbers moved three times
in one session. The gate names that exact base and requires the table re-derived
at the candidate acceptance binds. A count that has moved is a re-derivation and
not a scope change; a case that has vanished is.

Two things this milestone does not claim are worth naming, because both are easy
to overclaim. Outcome 1 does not make the audit findings correct — it makes
their repairs undeletable. And no probe in this gate proves that a regression
corpus is honest; that stays an independent-review judgment, which is exactly
why Outcome 7 exists and why its repairs carry mutation evidence rather than a
passing run.

One recorded `M2` limitation is **withdrawn rather than inherited**, and saying
so is part of this milestone rather than a footnote about the last one. The
cleanup period is session configuration after all: `cleanup_grace_ms` flows from
`Loopex.start_link/1` and `loopex run --cleanup-grace-ms` through
`LoopexComposition.start/1` to both the session and the executor, survives
journal reconstruction, and is reported on every run terminal record and
`run.finished` event. Accepted ADR 0009 is met. What survives in that
neighbourhood is not an ADR 0009 obligation at all: the program the executor asks
whether a process group still has members is a start option defaulting to
`/bin/ps`, and on an image that ships `ps` elsewhere every cancel and deadline
ending reports `outcome_unknown`. That is truthful and useless, and making it
unnecessary is a portability decision — `/proc`, `kill(pid, 0)` over a retained
group, or a platform syscall — rather than plumbing. `M3` does not take that
decision and carries no outcome for it. It is named here so that withdrawing the
cleanup-period limitation is not read as closing something it never covered.

<a id="concept-plan-scope"></a>
### Scope

Product code intended to survive the milestone: the required-only lower-bound
resolution in the context-admission path; a dispatcher that answers from
deferred reads rather than synchronous Store calls inside its own call handler,
with the queue, overflow, and invalidation ordering that requires; and a
journal-derived retention bound for spent provider attempts. Nothing else. No
new application, no new port, no new public function on the `Loopex` facade, and
no new operator affordance.

Repository-owned enforcement intended to survive the milestone: one closed-gate
aggregate driven by the canonical register, the structural check that proves
every milestone gate invokes it, and the corrected `M0` outcome-1 verdicts.

Governance work intended to survive the milestone: the ADR 0002 amendment that
raises the floor, the four gate transactions that rebind `.tool-versions`, the
`M2` gate generation that rebinds the isolation corpus, and this gate lock over
the seventy-nine inherited regression cases.

Everything else is out. The app-server, any protocol, any transport, any new
client, session fork, compaction, model switching, a daemon, concurrent clients,
a store migration, a performance budget, a public compatibility freeze, and the
`0.1.0` tag all belong to `M4` or later. The retained `M4` draft in
[the archive](../archive/M4.md) is design input, not scope.

No checker, register row, or evidence record substitutes for the three product
repairs and the running aggregate. A component that does not serve one of the
eight outcomes or an explicitly locked supporting mechanism is out of scope.

<a id="concept-plan-non-goals"></a>
### Non-Goals

`M3` ships no headless application boundary, protocol, schema bundle, golden
vectors, sample clients, durable interaction lifecycle, app-server, daemon,
socket, network transport, concurrent clients, controller leases, takeover,
residency, or cross-process cancellation. Those are `M4` and `M5`, and the
proposed decisions that carry them —
[ADR 0019](../adr/0019-experimental-public-session-protocol.md#concept) and
[ADR 0020](../adr/0020-durable-interaction-lifecycle-and-host-policy-authority.md#concept) —
stay Proposed throughout and are not prerequisites for this milestone.

`M3` takes no new external dependency, publishes nothing, tags nothing, freezes
nothing, and moves no application version. It does not settle the
process-group-confirmation mechanism, does not open the generic context
pipeline, does not add a generic transport behaviour, and does not turn the
closed-gate aggregate into a general continuous-integration framework.

It also reopens no `M2` finding that an audit refuted against the code. A
finding that did not hold was refuted with its code path rather than patched,
and this milestone inherits the refutations exactly as it inherits the repairs.

## Workstreams

Ownership and the required rejoin order are fixed in the
[technical envelope](M3-technical.md#technical-plan-ownership).

- **A — Floor and gate transactions.** The ADR 0002 amendment, the four
  sequenced transactions that rebind `.tool-versions`, the `M2` generation that
  rebinds the isolation corpus, and the `M0` outcome-1 verdict correction.
  Nothing else may change a bound artifact while these are in flight, so this
  rejoins first and alone.
- **B — Closed-gate aggregate.** The register-driven aggregate, its structural
  invocation check, and the bootstrap wiring. It rejoins second, because every
  later workstream evidence is worth more once a closed gate is actually run.
- **C — Product repairs.** ADR 0017 step 5, the dispatcher deferred reads, and
  the spent-attempt retention bound: three independent slices in core. They
  rejoin third, in any internal order.
- **D — Corpus honesty and the inherited lock.** The eight frozen weaknesses,
  the two partial-evidence items, and this gate lock over the seventy-nine
  inherited regression cases. It rejoins last, because a locked name cannot be
  fixed after it is locked and because its mutants must be taken against the
  repaired product rather than the pre-repair one.
